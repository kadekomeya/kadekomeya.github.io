## Optimization... For Humans?
Some people may say the slowest element of programming is the human programmer.  I give you a solution that is better than just replacing the programmer with AI, but rather improving the programmer that already exists with a review on question quality assurance.  Eric Steven Raymond precisely iterates over this in their article "How To Ask Questions The Smart Way".  For this comparison I chose a broad search term "How do I make my code faster" since as a game modder, I typically search things like this but much more constrained very often when programming.  One of the most recent mods I am working on demands as much performance as possible because there are many objects in the scene. I don't post on StackOverflow simply because I usually piece together information from multiple searches for what I am looking for.  The best solutions seem to already be answered a fair time ago, but they are always in parts in multiple different places.  Searching for multiple sources also helps me with identifying credibility of the sources.

## Saving the User Interface
https://stackoverflow.com/questions/45893768/how-do-i-find-out-what-parts-of-my-code-are-inefficient-in-python
This post is a good example of what Raymond expects in a StackOverflow question.  The user clearly defines what language they are using in the header. The user then goes over what they have already tried in the order they have tried them in the post itself, clarifying what the user knows and doesn't know.  What's even more fitting is that this is and extension question of what they had asked about prior, making this post the deeper specifics into what they are trying to do.  The user also links the posts they looked over prior which has even more context to what they are trying to solve.  Finally they end their post with two clearly defined questions that are easy to identify, so that helpers can quickly respond and the context is easy to follow for future readers.

## Fighting the User Interface
https://stackoverflow.com/questions/46647152/how-can-i-make-this-code-faster
Here in this post we have a bad example.  The user is asking the community on how to make their code faster, but they don't specify what they want faster so the header is too broad.  They only define it in the post itself in a misleading way and not in the header. Furthermore in the comment section their post devolves into arguing over what the post itself is about without even telling what the language they are using is, and even another commenter is able to come up with a better name for the post, yet the post title remains unchanged and very broad.  As such the post even recieved negative ratings to reflect not only the helpers' frustration, but also the viewers' confusion as well.
