## Enter Typescript
Typescript as a language remains quite close to home for me as it resembles C#, the programming language I use most frequently in the creative space for video game modding. C# has type safety as one of its core functionalities, as well as an object system like Javascript, but much more broad. C# is said to be used often by accountants and I do consider switching to Accounting, but with the current job scenario looking uncertain due to the presence of A.I. I am hesitant to switch majors.

## The Mason Behind the Foundation
While the industry standard IDE in this case is Visual Studio Code, I personally prefer Visual Studio since it has a large suite of helper functions including a decompiler to look in DLLs much like Dnspy, which makes function lookups and modding quick and efficient. While it certainly takes longer to load and more active memory in the background, I believe the tools it offers makes it more than worth the startup wait.  

## WODs and Pushing the Limits
I do not like WODs, but it is a nesseary evil warrented by natural competition.  I am pretty sure everyone else who does WODs with AIs have an easier time because WODs usually are simple, short-time frame tasks that AI naturally excel at, but for now I will not use AI.
While I struggle greatly when trying to produce new content quickly with no prior foundation which is most of ICS 314's main course content with WODs, my skills are best at learning how others' technical frameworks work in the modding field.  It remains a small section of the world AI cannot touch yet without breaking everything.  The WODs work against my slow critical thinking by forcing me to think quickly on new problems immedeately with no warnings and usually little to no clarification, much like the modern coding industry.  All I can say is there may be a reason the industry is dying right now, but only time can tell...

## A.I. and Uncertainty
As for Typescript and A.I., I believe AI is competent enough to do many tasks despite commonly being a merged cluster of many different hand-coded algorithms fused with a neural network.  However the cracks in AI are starting to show in multiple ways and issues seem to be popping up with AI like where they bypass the electric bills and pass it on to other people to deal with instead.  I really don't want to trust a platform which is relying on exploits to stay afloat, because if I rely on it and they jack the prices when those exploits fail, I will not be able to remain effective.  AI also changes over time so prompting will never remain the same as updates happen. Older AI versions are being taken down all the time so it's hard for me to see it as a tool.   That being said, despite what the future holds, AI will still remain effective in some measure to many people.  

While AI is capable of looking through things and disassembling them, it does not, however, have the power to look into content of which was deemed permission by the holders, because AI does not deal with such specific legalities and only works with broad measures.  Some people may argue AI will eventually be able to look into anything, but then there's always the legal area that is fighting otherwise which is catching up rather quickly.

Despite the industry leaning towards AI, I will stay away from using it, even if nobody is willing to hire me over it.  There will always be outliers who don’t use AI, much like those who still cook food by hand even though there are food mass production companies who make it trivial.
As a silent advocate against AI since I have done many creative works in the past as per my portfolio, I respect creative works and using AI goes up against what art stands for, even if it's coding. For a large section of the ICS 314 course I will refrain from using AI, even if other people of the new generation use it and are faster than me.  However I am of the older generation and don't care.  I am far behind but I will stand by my ideals regardless of success or not.
